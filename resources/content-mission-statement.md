## Mission Statement (Main Body)

Fingerhut Law exists to represent workers who have been discriminated against, harassed, retaliated against, and wrongfully terminated — and to hold their employers accountable in court.

I believe victims. I stand with victims.

When a worker walks into my office, they are usually walking in at one of the most vulnerable points in their professional life. They have already been disbelieved by an HR department, ignored by a manager, or punished for speaking up. They do not need another person treating their story as something to be filtered, doubted, or minimized. They need an attorney who will listen carefully, take their account seriously, and then go to work.

No amount of money can undo what happened. We can't rewind time. But money is the civil remedy this system provides — and getting my client every dollar that the law makes available is the most concrete way I know to translate a wrong into something that reshapes a person's life.

I started Fingerhut Law after eleven years of representing employees — including years at one of New York's largest plaintiffs-only employment firms — because I wanted to build a practice that runs on the values I have come to believe in most: belief in the client, preparation that anticipates everything, and the willingness to take a case to verdict when that is what the case requires.

## "What This Firm Stands For" (optional short list, homepage-ready)

- **Belief, first.** The client is not auditioning for representation. If a worker tells me what happened, my starting position is that I believe them.
- **Preparation that anticipates everything.** Court is not the place to be caught off guard about a detail. I work cases from end to end so that nothing in the file is unfamiliar.
- **Willingness to try the case.** Settlement is often the right outcome — but only when the defense knows the case will be tried if it is not resolved fairly.
- **Plain talk.** Employment law is full of jargon. My clients should always understand what is happening in their case, what their options are, and why.
- **Real respect for the client.** Some clients are polished. Some are not. Some have lived through things that have made it hard to trust attorneys, especially attorneys who look like me. None of that affects the quality of representation they will receive at this firm.

## Editorial / Design Notes

- **Voice:** First-person ("I"), never "we." This is consistent with the firm-naming constraint — the firm is currently solo, and saying "we" implies a team that doesn't exist. First-person is also the more credible register for a senior practitioner and aligns with the Wang Hecker boutique aesthetic identified in `competitive-research.md`.
- **Tone:** Calm and confident in the headline; direct and fierce in the pull quotes. Avoid combat slogans ("toughest in the city," "fight to the death"). The Wigdor register is wrong for a NYC solo. Outten & Golden / Wang Hecker registers are right.
- **Avoid:** stock photos of gavels, scales of justice, or "diverse team in a conference room" imagery. Per `competitive-research.md`, use either (a) a clean professional portrait of Steven against a neutral background or (b) an NYC architectural detail.
- **Trust signal placement:** "$30M+ recovered for workers" and "Super Lawyers Rising Star (2018–2025)" belong adjacent to this mission section. The mission gives the *why*; the trust numbers give the *credentialing*. They work together.
