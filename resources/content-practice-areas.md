## The Practice Areas

### 1. Sexual Harassment and Sexual Assault at Work

I represent women and men who have been sexually harassed, sexually assaulted, propositioned, pressured into affairs by supervisors, subjected to unwanted touching, retaliated against for refusing sexual advances, or forced out of their jobs after reporting harassment.

A lot of these cases involve a profound power imbalance — the harasser is the CEO, the partner, the director, the manager who controls the worker's livelihood. When that imbalance exists, there is no meaningful consent to a sexual relationship; the law in New York understands this, and so do I.

I have tried sexual harassment cases to verdict in both state and federal court, including a unanimous jury verdict in Queens County Supreme Court for a young woman of color sexually harassed at a Subway franchise *(Grant v. Pexie Enterprises)*, and a federal verdict in the Eastern District of New York that I defended on appeal in the Second Circuit *(Smart v. USA Labor for Hire)*.

**New York protections include:** Title VII of the Civil Rights Act, the New York State Human Rights Law, the New York City Human Rights Law (the most protective in the country), and the New York Adult Survivors Act.

### 2. Pregnancy Discrimination and Caregiver Retaliation

I take pregnancy discrimination cases personally. Workers who are pregnant, or who have just given birth, carry one of the most significant burdens a person can carry — and they deserve more accommodation from their employers, not less.

I represent employees who have been demoted, passed over, harassed, denied accommodations, or terminated because of pregnancy, childbirth, lactation, IVF treatment, or related medical conditions. I also represent workers retaliated against for requesting maternity or parental leave, for taking time off for a child's medical needs, or for requesting reasonable accommodations like a private place to pump.

**New York protections include:** the Pregnancy Discrimination Act, the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act, Title VII, the New York State and NYC Human Rights Laws, and New York Paid Family Leave.

### 3. New York Paid Family Leave (PFL) Violations

*Render this section near the bottom of the page (position 12 per the ordering note above). This is a **gateway area**, not a primary intake driver — when workers contact the firm about a PFL grievance, the consultation evaluates whether there is a more substantial underlying claim (pregnancy discrimination, disability discrimination, FMLA retaliation, hostile work environment) that the PFL issue may be a symptom of. Page copy should reflect that, as below.*

New York Paid Family Leave is one of the strongest paid-leave programs in the country, and it is still new enough that many employers ignore it or get it wrong. Workers can take PFL to bond with a new child, to care for a family member with a serious health condition, or to manage family obligations when a relative is called to active military service.

I successfully litigated one of New York State's earliest Paid Family Leave cases — *In Regard to Stacey E. Johnson*, PFL Case No. 000009 — securing monetary recovery for a client wrongfully terminated from Vassar Brothers Medical Center while on approved leave caring for her infant son who was undergoing emergency surgery.

**An important note about how PFL cases work.** Standalone PFL retaliation matters are litigated through New York's Workers' Compensation Board, where attorney's fees must be approved by the Board and are capped by statute. That structure makes purely PFL-only cases a narrow recovery path.

**But a PFL violation rarely happens in isolation.** When an employer terminates, demotes, or retaliates against a worker for taking PFL, that same conduct is almost always also actionable as **pregnancy discrimination, disability discrimination, FMLA retaliation, or hostile work environment** — under federal, New York State, and New York City statutes that carry far stronger remedies. If your employer treated you badly around a PFL claim, that may be the visible symptom of a much larger case. Talk to me before assuming the only path forward is a PFL hearing.

If your employer terminated, demoted, or retaliated against you because you took PFL — or because of the circumstances that led you to need PFL — contact Fingerhut Law for a free, confidential consultation.

### 4. Race Discrimination

I represent Black, Latino, Asian, and other workers of color who have been subjected to racial slurs, denied promotions, paid less, written up for things their white coworkers were not written up for, and terminated under pretextual reasons that mask racial animus.

In one of my most significant settlements, I represented eleven construction workers in a race discrimination case where two supervisors regularly used racial slurs against the crew. The case settled for **$2,000,000** — but only after I made absolutely clear that we were prepared to go to trial.

**New York protections include:** Title VII, 42 U.S.C. § 1981, the New York State and NYC Human Rights Laws.

---

### 5. Retaliation

Retaliation cases are often the strongest cases I see. An employer's response to a worker's complaint about discrimination, harassment, wage theft, or unsafe conditions is frequently more provable than the underlying conduct — because retaliation creates a paper trail.

I represent workers who have been fired, demoted, written up, transferred, isolated, or otherwise punished for:
- Complaining about discrimination or harassment
- Cooperating with an internal HR investigation
- Filing an EEOC, NYSDHR, or NYCCHR charge
- Reporting wage and hour violations
- Reporting unsafe working conditions
- Requesting an accommodation for a disability, pregnancy, or religious practice
- Taking protected leave

The law calls all of these "protected activity." Punishing a worker for engaging in protected activity is illegal — even when the underlying complaint turns out to be unfounded.

---

### 6. Wrongful Termination

"Wrongful termination" is not a standalone legal claim under New York law — but a termination that violates anti-discrimination, anti-retaliation, leave, or whistleblower statutes is unlawful, and the right framework matters.

I represent workers terminated because of race, sex, age, disability, religion, sexual orientation, gender identity, pregnancy, or national origin; workers terminated in retaliation for protected activity; workers terminated after requesting an accommodation; and workers terminated in violation of public policy.

If you were fired and something about it does not feel right, talk to an attorney before signing anything — particularly a severance agreement with a release of claims.

---

### 7. Hostile Work Environment

A hostile work environment claim arises when discriminatory conduct is so severe or pervasive that it alters the conditions of employment. Under the **New York City Human Rights Law**, the standard is even more employee-protective than under federal Title VII — a single sufficiently severe incident, or a pattern of conduct that a reasonable person would find more than a "petty slight," is enough.

I represent workers subjected to hostile work environments on the basis of sex, race, age, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, national origin, and pregnancy.

---

### 8. LGBTQ+ Workplace Discrimination

I represent gay, lesbian, bisexual, transgender, and non-binary employees who have been harassed, discriminated against, denied benefits, or terminated because of who they are.

I wrote about LGBT+ workplace protections in *Lawyer Monthly* (July 2019 cover feature) — discussing how the New York City Human Rights Law's protections for sexual orientation and gender identity meaningfully exceed federal protections, and the kinds of harassment that are actionable in New York.

**Protections include:** Title VII (as interpreted in *Bostock v. Clayton County*), the New York State and NYC Human Rights Laws.

---

### 9. Age Discrimination

I represent workers over 40 who have been pushed out of their jobs in favor of younger employees, denied promotions because of age, replaced after long tenures by less-experienced workers, or pressured into "early retirement" packages that are really just disguised terminations.

I have settled age discrimination cases for senior executives, including an **$800,000** settlement for a C-suite executive terminated because of his age.

**Protections include:** the Age Discrimination in Employment Act, the New York State and NYC Human Rights Laws (which extend protections more broadly than federal law).

---

### 10. Disability Discrimination and Failure to Accommodate

I represent workers with physical disabilities, mental health conditions, chronic illnesses, and pregnancy-related medical conditions who have been denied reasonable accommodations, harassed about their disabilities, or terminated because of them.

The most common pattern: a worker discloses a medical condition, requests an accommodation, and is then quietly managed out under a pretextual "performance" rationale. Document the request. Document the timeline. Then call.

**Protections include:** the Americans with Disabilities Act, the New York State and NYC Human Rights Laws.

---

### 11. Wage Theft and Unpaid Overtime

I represent workers who have not been paid the wages they earned — including unpaid overtime, misclassification as exempt or as independent contractors, withheld tips, off-the-clock work, and unlawful deductions.

**Protections include:** the Fair Labor Standards Act, the New York Labor Law (with longer lookback periods and additional damages), and New York's wage notice and pay statement requirements.

### 12. Severance Negotiation and Separation Agreements

*Render at position 11 per the ordering note. **Secondary practice area** — the firm reviews and negotiates severance agreements for the right matters, but is not actively marketing to high-volume severance-shopper intake. Do not feature this area in homepage tiles or top-of-funnel content.*

If you have been offered a severance agreement, do not sign it without talking to an employment lawyer first. The release of claims in a typical severance agreement is broad — it can extinguish discrimination, harassment, and retaliation claims that may be worth significantly more than the severance number on the table.

I review and negotiate severance agreements for executives and senior professionals, often securing significantly improved terms — higher payment, longer continuation of benefits, agreed-upon references, and modifications to non-compete and non-disparagement provisions. I am particularly interested in severance reviews where the underlying termination appears to be discriminatory or retaliatory, since in those situations the release the employer is asking you to sign may be worth a great deal more in negotiation than it currently reflects.
