## The Values

### 1. I believe my clients.

When a worker comes to me and says they were harassed, discriminated against, or fired for the wrong reason, my starting position is that I believe them. Defendants will spend enormous resources accusing my clients of lying — calling them gold-diggers, fame-seekers, or worse. That is gaslighting. My clients are not coming forward for money or attention; they are coming forward because something real happened to them at work, and the law says they have a right to do something about it.

### 2. No client is judged at this firm.

Some clients are polished. Many are not. Some have lived through things that have made it hard to trust attorneys — especially attorneys who look like me. Some have made decisions in their personal or professional lives that, with the benefit of hindsight, they would do differently. None of that affects the quality of representation they will receive here.

The saying is that you should not judge a person until you have walked a mile in their shoes. Honestly, a mile is not enough. I am not going to pretend I can fully understand where every client has been before they walked into my office. What I can promise is that no one at Fingerhut Law will ever make a client feel small, naive, or unworthy for what brought them through the door.

### 3. Preparation that anticipates everything.

Court is not the place to be caught off guard about a detail in your own case. I work matters from end to end so that nothing in the file is unfamiliar — every email, every text, every conversation, every prior position taken by the employer. Judges notice. Defense attorneys notice. Most importantly, juries notice. Over-preparation is the only kind of preparation that exists at this firm.

### 4. Willingness to try the case.

Most employment cases settle. They settle because both sides understand that going to trial is uncertain and expensive — and that calculus only works if the defense actually believes the plaintiff's lawyer is ready to try the case. I am ready to try the case. I have done it in state court and in federal court, and I have defended those verdicts on appeal in the Second Circuit. That readiness is what produces the settlement numbers my clients deserve.

### 5. The client is informed.

Employment law is full of jargon — Title VII, NYSHRL, NYCHRL, EEOC, NYSDHR, preponderance of the evidence, summary judgment, motion in limine. My clients should always know what is happening in their case, what their realistic options are, and why I am recommending what I am recommending. If a client does not understand the strategy, that is on me, not on them.

### 6. Tough when it needs to be, professional always.

A lot of defense attorneys are willing to work cooperatively, build a real professional relationship, and get a case resolved. Many of my best adversarial relationships look like that, and I work to build them whenever I can — go high if they go low, be the lawyer they want to call back next time.

Some defense attorneys want a dogfight. When that is the situation, I do not back down. My clients are entitled to representation that matches the intensity coming at them.

### 7. Boutique focus, real attention.

This is a focused employment law practice. My clients are not files passed between associates. When a worker hires Fingerhut Law, they get me — directly, on the phone, in mediations, in depositions, in court. That is the point of the firm.

## Editorial / Design Notes

- **Voice:** First-person throughout. Never "we" / "our attorneys" / "our team."
- **Tone:** Empathetic and assertive — the same balance Smith Mullin and Vladeck strike (per `competitive-research.md`). Avoid the Wigdor "toughest firm in the country" register; that does not match Steven's actual voice, which is fiercer about *believing* clients than about *destroying* employers.
- **Layout suggestion:** Numbered list with generous whitespace. Each value gets a short H3 plus 2–4 sentences. Resist the urge to make this longer — boutique sites breathe.
- **Avoid icons.** Most law firms put a generic icon (gavel, briefcase, balance scales) next to each value. This is the universal "cheap" tell from the competitive research. Type-only treatment, with strong hierarchy, reads more sophisticated.
- **Where on the site:** This works best as a homepage section (between the hero and the practice areas), or as the second half of the About page. Do not bury it under multiple clicks.
