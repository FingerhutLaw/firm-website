### Federal Jury Verdict — Race and Sex Discrimination, Retaliation, Defamation

**$425,000+** — January 2024

**Smart v. USA Labor for Hire, Inc., et al.**
Case No. 1:20-cv-5594 (E.D.N.Y.)

After a federal jury trial in the Eastern District of New York, Steven obtained a verdict in excess of $425,000 for a client subjected to a hostile work environment, retaliation, and defamation. The client had been discriminated against on the basis of her sex and race, and was terminated in retaliation for objecting to the harassment.

After trial, Steven defeated the defendants' motion for remittitur and motion for a new trial, and prevailed on a robust application for attorney's fees, which the court granted without reduction.

### State Jury Verdict — Sexual Harassment, Assault, Battery, Constructive Discharge

**$112,000** — March 2022

**Grant v. Pexie Enterprises, Inc.**
Index No. 704041/2015 (Queens County Supreme Court)

In a jury trial in Queens County Supreme Court — conducted in 2022 under COVID-19 mask mandates that required the jury, the parties, witnesses, and counsel to wear masks throughout the proceedings — Steven obtained a unanimous verdict for a young woman of color who had been sexually harassed, assaulted, and battered by her supervisor at a Subway franchise restaurant, and who was constructively discharged after fewer than two weeks of employment. The jury rejected the defendants' two frivolous counterclaims (abuse of process and fraud) on a 31-page verdict sheet.

---

### Paid Family Leave Hearing — One of New York's First

**Recovery for Wrongfully Terminated Employee** — May 2021

**In Regard to Stacey E. Johnson**
PFL Case No. 000009

Steven successfully litigated one of New York State's earliest Paid Family Leave matters, securing monetary compensation for a client who was wrongfully terminated from Vassar Brothers Medical Center while on approved leave caring for her infant son, who was undergoing emergency surgery. The hearing — conducted over months in virtual proceedings — involved both PFL violations and a separate race-retaliation claim that ultimately resolved through settlement following the PFL ruling.

## Editorial / Design Notes

- **Trust signal in the hero.** Place the **"$30M+ recovered for workers"** anchor stat at the top of the page in display type. Per `competitive-research.md` §3, every top firm has one headline number — this is the firm's.
- **Card hierarchy.** Verdicts and appellate results > settlements. Verdicts are public, named, defensible. Settlements are anonymized aggregates. The page should read in that order so the most credible material is first.
- **No client photographs, no client names.** Even with permission, publishing identifying details of plaintiffs is a closer call ethically and reputationally than most firms appreciate. Stay anonymized on settlements; use only the public caption on litigated matters.
- **Prior-results disclaimer in two places.** Once near the top of the page (above the verdicts), once at the bottom near the CTA. Plus the site-wide footer.
- **Avoid medallion/badge treatments.** No "Top Verdict 2024" badges, no "Million Dollar Advocates Forum" badges next to the dollar amounts. Let the numbers stand on their own. Badges next to verdicts read like personal injury TV-ad aesthetics — wrong register for a NYC employment boutique.
- **Internal links.** Each card should be linkable to (or co-located with) the relevant practice-area page so a reader interested in "Race Discrimination" can jump straight to that practice area from the $2M card.
- **Per `competitive-research.md` §5.** Wigdor uses "Results" as the primary CTA above "Contact." Steven should consider whether the homepage hero CTA should be **"See Results →"** in addition to (or instead of) "Schedule a Consultation." Results-first signals confidence and is more credible than another contact form for boutique-aesthetic firms. Recommendation: keep the consultation CTA as primary, add Results as a clearly visible secondary link in the hero — the boutique register favors a calmer CTA than Wigdor's.
