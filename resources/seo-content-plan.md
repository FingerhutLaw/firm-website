# SEO Content Plan — fingerhutlawgroup.com

A complete plan for the words and pages that will get NYC employment-law clients to find Fingerhut Law Group on Google.

This plan is grounded in three constraints:
1. **Solo NYC plaintiff-side firm.** Cannot compete with Outten & Golden (140 attorneys, decades of backlinks) on head terms like "employment lawyer NYC" within year one. Can compete — and win — on long-tail, NY-statute-specific queries.
2. **Boutique aesthetic.** No borough doorway pages ("Brooklyn employment lawyer," "Bronx employment lawyer"), no AI-spun blog content, no keyword stuffing. Per `competitive-research.md`, this reads SEO-thirsty and cheap.
3. **Plaintiff-side, worker-facing.** Content speaks to *the worker*, not to other lawyers. Employer-side and HR-facing queries are outside the practice.

---

## 1. Strategy in One Paragraph

The competitive moat is **New York–specific employment law expertise** — particularly the New York City Human Rights Law (NYCHRL), which is the most employee-protective statute in the country, plus New York State PFL, the NYS Human Rights Law as recently amended, and New York Labor Law wage protections. Most informational content on employment law that ranks today is national, written for a Title VII / EEOC reader. Steven has eleven years of practiced expertise in the NYC-specific layer that makes New York different. Owning that niche, through deep practice-area pages plus a steady cadence of long-tail NY-specific informational posts, is how a solo firm builds organic visibility without trying to outspend Outten & Golden on link-building.

## 5. Page Structure (template each page should follow)

### Practice-area pillar pages (~1,000–2,000 words)
1. **H1**: "[Practice Area] in New York" or "NYC [Practice Area] Lawyer"
2. **Hero**: 1–2 sentence positioning + phone CTA + scheduling CTA
3. **What is [practice area]?** (~150 words plain-English definition)
4. **Who is protected** (federal + NYS + NYC HRL)
5. **What counts as a violation** (4–6 concrete examples)
6. **NY-specific protections** *(the moat — call out NYCHRL specifically)*
7. **What to do if this is happening to you** (step-by-step)
8. **Statute of limitations / deadlines** (with explicit numbers)
9. **Possible recovery** (compensatory, emotional distress, punitive, fees)
10. **Notable matter** (Steven's relevant settlement or verdict)
11. **"Has this happened to you?"** scenario carousel (Outten & Golden pattern)
12. **Internal links** to 3–5 relevant blog posts
13. **CTA**: free consultation form + phone

### Blog posts (~1,200–2,500 words)
1. **H1** that matches the target keyword closely
2. **Author byline**: "By Steven Fingerhut" with link to /about
3. **TL;DR** (~75 words, in a callout box) — the answer for the reader who won't scroll
4. **Body** with H2 headings that match People-Also-Ask questions on Google
5. **NY-specific section** every time — this is what distinguishes the post from national content
6. **Internal links** to the relevant practice-area pillar
7. **CTA** at the bottom: short, "If [scenario], contact the firm for a free consultation"
8. **Updated date** prominently displayed (Google rewards freshness on legal content)

### 8a. What to do *before* having a leased physical office

These are safe to do immediately — none require a verifiable street address with signage:

- [ ] **NAP consistency** (Name, Phone, *eventually* Address) — identical wording across the website footer, every directory listing, and every press feature, so that when a real office is added later, every listing updates cleanly.
- [ ] **Bar directory listings** (free, high authority — these accept attorneys without a brick-and-mortar requirement):
  - NELA member directory
  - New York State Bar Association directory
  - New York City Bar Association directory
  - New Jersey State Bar Association directory
- [ ] **General legal directories** (free profiles — claim them even if you don't display badges per the competitive research's advice against badge soup):
  - Justia
  - FindLaw
  - Avvo (claim profile but don't display the badge on the site)
  - Martindale-Hubbell
  - Lawyers.com
- [ ] **Backlinks from press features** — make sure *Lawyer Monthly*, *Authority Magazine*, and *Beyond Exclamation* links to the firm site (not to Steven's old Phillips & Associates page)

### 8b. Hold for after the office lease is signed: Google Business Profile

**Do not file the Google Business Profile (GBP) for Fingerhut Law Group until Steven has a real, leased, signed-up office space.**

The reason is risk asymmetry. Google's Business Profile policy requires the listed address to be a place where the business is **physically present and serves customers during stated hours** — not a virtual office, not a mail drop, not a coworking hot desk visited occasionally. Law firms are also a Google-designated **high-spam category**: legal listings get reviewed and suspended more aggressively than most categories because the category gets gamed heavily by spammers and lead-generation operators.

If a virtual or mail-drop address is flagged — either by Google's automated review or by a competitor reporting it — the consequences can be:

- **Soft suspension:** listing goes dark; appeal is possible but slow.
- **Hard suspension:** the **business name + address combination** gets flagged as a policy violation in Google's records. Reinstatement is difficult, sometimes weeks of back-and-forth with Google support, and in worst-case outcomes practitioners report needing to either find a verifiably-staffed location and reapply *or* operate under a modified business name (e.g., "Fingerhut Employment Law" rather than "Fingerhut Law Group, PLLC") to get out from under the flag.
- **Collateral damage:** a suspended GBP can ripple into reduced organic visibility for the firm's overall name in search, because Google has flagged the entity.

The upside of filing GBP early is small (a few months of local-pack visibility for queries like "employment lawyer near me"). The downside of filing it wrong is potentially the **business name itself**. The trade is bad.

**Wait until:**
- A physical, leased office space is secured (not virtual, not mail forwarding, not occasional desk at a coworking space)
- Signage can be placed on or near the door identifying *Fingerhut Law Group*
- Real, honest, staffed business hours can be stated
- Photos of the actual exterior, interior, and signage exist

**Then claim the profile with:**
- **Business name:** Fingerhut Law Group, PLLC (exact match to NY corporate filing — do not embellish with keywords like "Fingerhut Law Group — Employment Lawyer NYC," which is also a policy violation)
- **Primary category:** Employment attorney
- **Secondary category:** Civil law attorney
- **Address:** the leased office address
- **Hours:** what is honest (likely "Mon–Fri, 9am–6pm ET, after-hours by appointment")
- **Photos:** exterior with signage, interior, Steven's professional headshot, NYC skyline. *Real* photos — not stock.
- **Service area:** New York City + 5 boroughs
- **Posts cadence:** weekly first month after publishing, then monthly

### 8c. Local citations to defer (Yelp, BBB)

Yelp and Better Business Bureau both list addresses prominently and are crawled by competitors. Defer these until after the office lease is signed for the same reason as GBP.

## 9. Tracking, Measurement, and Iteration

### Tools to install at launch
- [ ] **Google Search Console** (free): see what queries surface the site
- [ ] **Google Analytics 4** (free): traffic, sources, conversions
- [ ] **Squarespace Analytics**: built-in, sufficient for first 6 months
- [ ] **Conversion tracking**: contact form submission + phone-tap-to-call event

### Year-one targets (realistic for a NYC solo)
- **Month 3**: 100–250 organic visitors/month, indexing complete, Search Console showing impressions on long-tail queries
- **Month 6**: 400–700 organic visitors/month, first few queries ranking page 1, 2–5 form submissions/month
- **Month 12**: 800–1,500 organic visitors/month, ranking page 1 on a handful of practice-area keywords (likely PFL, severance, and one or two NYCHRL-specific queries first), 5–15 qualified leads/month

### What to monitor monthly
- Top 10 Search Console queries by impressions — write more content for those
- Top 10 by clicks but low position — improve the existing page
- Pages with high impressions but low CTR — rewrite the title and meta description
- Contact form sources (organic vs. referral vs. direct)

### What to do quarterly
- Refresh the top 3 highest-trafficked posts with current law and current date
- Add 1 new internal link from each new post back to its pillar page
- Audit dead links / broken images
- Check site speed (Squarespace can quietly slow down with image bloat)

## 10. What This Plan Does Not Cover (intentionally)

- **Paid search (Google Ads).** Worth considering after organic baselines exist (month 6+). Plaintiffs employment is competitive — CPC for "employment lawyer NYC" is often $20–$50. Skip until intake flow proves itself organically.
- **Social media.** LinkedIn presence as Steven personally is high-leverage and low effort; firm social accounts on TikTok/Instagram are not worth the time at this stage.
- **Backlink outreach campaigns.** The press features Steven already has are stronger than anything cold outreach would earn. Focus on writing one Authority-Magazine-quality piece per quarter instead.
- **AI-generated content tools.** Do not use them for the practice-area pages or the blog. Legal content is YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) under Google's quality guidelines — Google scrutinizes it for E-E-A-T signals, and AI-spun legal content is the textbook negative signal. Every word should be Steven's, or written by someone who has read this plan and is editing Steven's audio/notes.

## 11. Quick-Start Order of Operations

If you do nothing else, do these in this order:

1. **Build the 22 pre-launch pages** from section 3.
2. **Submit sitemap to Google Search Console** within 24 hours of launch.
3. **Write the 8 Phase 1 blog posts** over the first 90 days (~1 per 11 days).
4. **Update NELA and bar directory listings** to point to the new site (not the old firm).
5. **Claim free profiles on Justia, FindLaw, Avvo, Martindale, Lawyers.com** (no address required for basic listings).
6. **Wait 90 days** before judging organic performance. SEO takes time; don't churn the plan in week 3.
7. **Defer until office lease is signed:** Google Business Profile, Yelp, BBB (see §8b for why).
